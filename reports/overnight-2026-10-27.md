## Known gaps and risks

- The first run stopped during turn 3 streaming, but the two-opponent run
  completed its 12-turn bound. A typical full-game success and long-game
  multi-opponent competence remain unproven; compaction has not been exercised
  by this run.
- Long model calls may still look idle unless the event log exposes request,
  streamed/raw output, tool calls, repairs, and final action decisions promptly.
- A valid action batch is not the same as a good strategic turn. Review both
  game-state effects and the model's visible reasoning/output.
- Candidate omission is currently a measurable coverage risk: the baseline
  1200-token table offered 73 candidates and disclosed 60 omitted, with
  unit-first ordering potentially starving city, technology, or diplomacy
  choices. The audit did not establish that this caused a bad decision; any
  policy change must be versioned and compared under fixed map/difficulty
  settings.
- The two-opponent transcript exposed a normal intra-batch dependency: a road
  action offered before a same-batch city foundation became stale and was
  rejected safely after refresh. Consider an explicit batch-dependency hint in
  the prompt later, but do not classify this verified safety behavior as a
  harness failure.
- Token/cost and cache claims must come from the provider response/event log;
  do not estimate them from wall-clock time.
- Any run using degraded behavior, repair caps, or a restarted container should
  be labeled accordingly rather than compared as a clean game.
